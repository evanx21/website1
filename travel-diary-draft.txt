DAY 1 — ARRIVING ON OʻAHU
We landed on Oʻahu in the afternoon, and I took a couple of photos at the airport before our trip really began. After leaving the airport, we went to Ala Moana to pick up our rental car. We had missed lunch, so our first proper stop was for musubi and a few snacks. It felt good to finally sit down and eat after traveling.

Later, we drove to Waikīkī to enjoy the sun and take photos by the water. Seeing the ocean and Diamond Head made the first day feel real at last. That evening, we wandered through some shops near Waikīkī Beach before heading back to rest.

DAY 2 — BREAKFAST, PINEAPPLE, AND TEPPANYAKI
The next morning, we ate at Island Vintage Coffee. We ordered an ube bagel, a savory rice-and-egg dish, and a mango smoothie. The bagel's purple color made it especially fun to photograph, and sitting down together for breakfast was a lovely way to begin the day. [Check the exact name of the rice dish before publishing; the photo alone does not confirm it.]

After breakfast, we drove toward the middle of the island to visit Dole Plantation. We tried fresh pineapple in a pineapple bowl and pineapple ice cream, then looked around for souvenirs. The pineapple tasted wonderfully fresh, and the whole stop put me in such a good mood. I also filmed some of the pineapple grown there.

In the evening, we went to Ala Moana Center for teppanyaki. The flames rising from the grill were exciting to watch, and the food smelled amazing. It was a delicious end to an already happy day.

DAY 3 — MUSUBI, PEARL HARBOR, AND UDON
We took the third morning a little more slowly and went to Musubi Cafe. We bought a whole tray of different musubi and had fun trying them together. After all that tasting, I still thought the classic kind was the best. [The separate Island Deli photo is a different musubi stop, not the Musubi Cafe tray.]

Later that same day, we visited Pearl Harbor and the USS Missouri. At Pearl Harbor, I learned more about the Japanese attack on December 7, 1941, which brought the United States into World War II. The USS Missouri has a different connection to the war: Japan's formal surrender was signed aboard the ship in Tokyo Bay in 1945. Standing near its enormous guns and walking past the flags made those events feel less distant. I left feeling that I had learned a lot; it was an eye-opening and thought-provoking visit.

That evening, my girlfriend, our friend, and I went to Marugame Udon in Waikīkī. We ordered several different udon bowls and tempura, so the table was crowded in the best way. The tempura had just come out of the fryer when I reached it in line, and it was especially crisp. The udon was smooth and springy. After waiting about half an hour, I was happy to say the meal was worth it.

DAY 4 — KUALOA RANCH AND SECRET ISLAND
On day four, we drove from Waikīkī to Kualoa Ranch for a full day at Secret Island. I had booked a little late, so I was initially disappointed that some of the other tours were full. But our day turned out to be a lovely mix of activities and time to relax.

In the morning, we tried stand-up paddleboarding and took a boat ride to look for turtles. Lunch was included, although it was probably the least exciting part of the day: a cold chicken sandwich, some chips, and fruit. It was simple and, honestly, not very tasty. After lunch, we paddled a canoe toward a sandbar and joined a pond tour. Between activities, I kept looking up at the green mountains and out across the water. Even with a disappointing lunch, I was having a wonderful time.

By the time we got back from Kualoa Ranch, we were exhausted. We picked up a simple dinner from the 7-Eleven across from our hotel, went back to our room, and called it a night.

DAY 5 — A QUIET LAST DAY AND A RAINBOW
After four full days of exploring Oʻahu, our fifth day was also our last. We had no tours or plans to fit in. We simply drove to Turtle Beach on the North Shore and spent some quiet time there, letting ourselves relax and enjoy the final day of the trip.

On the drive, we spotted a rainbow. I felt incredibly lucky to see it—I’ve always believed that a rainbow brings good luck. It was such a happy moment to carry home with me. And if you’re reading this, I hope I can share a little of that luck with you, too.

ENDING
Five days on Oʻahu went by much too quickly. We ate so much good food, tried new things, and still found moments to slow down by the water. The trip was full, fun, and genuinely refreshing. I keep thinking about the fresh pineapple, the crispy tempura, the quiet beach, and that rainbow on our last day.

There is so much more of the island I want to see, so I’m already planning to come back this Christmas. For now, I’m taking home the photos, the memories, and the feeling that five days was only the beginning.
